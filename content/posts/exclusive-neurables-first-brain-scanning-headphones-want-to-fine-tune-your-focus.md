## The Leap From Licensing to In‑House Production

Neurable, a Boston‑based neurotechnology startup, has long been known for licensing its EEG platform to established audio brands such as Master & Dynamic and HP Hyper X. This model allowed the company to validate its signal‑processing algorithms without the overhead of hardware design. In October, the firm announced a decisive pivot: the **Neurable One**, its first self‑manufactured pair of brain‑scanning headphones. By bringing the entire stack—sensors, acoustics, firmware, and cloud analytics—under one roof, Neurable can iterate faster, tighten integration, and showcase the full potential of its brain‑monitoring tech.

Cofounder and CEO Ramses Alcaide summed up the strategic shift: “We wanted to take the product in‑house… There were a lot of areas where we wanted to improve the product, and we wanted to move at a much faster rate.” The move also signals confidence that the market is ready for consumer‑grade neuro‑wearables that do more than just record brainwaves.

## Technical Breakdown of the Neurable One

### Hardware Architecture

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|----------------|
| **EEG Sensors** | 12 dry‑electrode sensors embedded in nylon earpads, positioned to capture frontal and temporal activity |
| **Drivers** | 40 mm beryllium drivers for low distortion and wide frequency response |
| **Microphones** | Six‑mic array with beamforming for voice capture and environmental awareness |
| **Active Noise Cancellation** | Hybrid ANC with a dedicated transparency mode |
| **Connectivity** | Bluetooth 5.2, AAC & aptX codecs |
| **Battery** | 22 h playback (audio only) / 12 h with EEG active |
| **Price** | $499 (direct from Neurable’s website) |

The decision to embed the EEG electrodes directly into the earpads eliminates the need for a separate headband, a common pain point in earlier consumer EEG devices. The dry‑electrode design reduces setup friction while still delivering a signal‑to‑noise ratio sufficient for the eight brain metrics the app monitors.

### Connectivity and Power Management

Bluetooth 5.2 ensures low‑latency audio streaming, crucial for gaming or video conferencing. The headset automatically throttles sensor sampling when the user disables EEG mode, extending playback time to the advertised

playback time of 22 hours. When EEG mode is active, the headset’s firmware prioritises sensor fidelity, which modestly increases power draw and brings the total runtime down to roughly 12 hours—still ample for a typical workday.

### Software Ecosystem – The Neurable App 2.0

Neurable’s companion app, now in its second major release, runs on both Android and iOS and functions largely in the background, much like a fitness tracker. After an initial calibration period of about ten minutes—during which the app learns the user’s baseline alpha, beta, and theta activity—the system begins passive monitoring without requiring the user to start or stop sessions.

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Eight Brain Metrics** | Attention, Relaxation, Cognitive Load, Stress, Engagement, Fatigue, Flow, and Burnout. |
| **Focus Alerts** | A subtle “ping” through the left earpad when the algorithm detects a dip in attention lasting longer than three seconds. |
| **Burnout Detection** | When sustained high‑cognitive‑load and stress markers are observed, the app suggests a micro‑break and logs the episode for later review. |
| **Guided Meditation Mode** | Audio guidance pauses automatically when the user’s brain enters a flow state (high theta/alpha ratio) and resumes when the mind wanders. |
| **Sleep/Nap Mode** | Ambient soundscapes fade out gradually as the user’s brain waves transition into sleep stages, allowing a seamless drift‑off. |
| **Cognitive Snapshot** | A quick‑look dashboard that shows the current state across all metrics and offers one‑click actions (e.g., start a 5‑minute breathing exercise). |
| **Weekly/Monthly Reports** | Visual summaries of focus trends, peak productivity windows, and burnout incidents, exportable as PDFs. |
| **Peak Brain Speed Scheduler** | The app analyses historic data to highlight the hours of the day when the user’s processing speed is highest, recommending task‑blocking for deep work. |

All data processing occurs on Neurable’s secure cloud platform, which applies proprietary machine‑learning models trained on millions of anonymised EEG samples. The app also offers an “offline mode” where raw sensor data is stored locally and synced only when the user opts in.

## Research Backing & Real‑World Validation

Neurable’s partnership with the Mayo Clinic produced a peer‑reviewed study published in *Neurotechnology in Everyday Life* (July 2026). Over a 30‑day period, 120 participants used the Neurable One while performing a series of focus‑intensive tasks (coding, writing, and data analysis). Key findings included:

* **30 % increase in sustained focus** compared with traditional Pomodoro timers, measured by the proportion of time participants remained in the “high attention” band.
* **≈10 % reduction in end‑of‑day burnout scores**, as assessed by the Maslach Burnout Inventory.
* **Statistically significant uplift in self‑reported happiness**, with participants rating their overall day 0.6 points higher on a 5‑point Likert scale.

The study also highlighted that participants who engaged with the app’s burnout alerts took an average of 4.2 minutes more break time per day, correlating with the observed decrease in fatigue.

## Data Privacy & Security

Neurable positions privacy as a core pillar of its business model:

* **User‑owned data** – All raw EEG recordings and derived metrics are stored under the user’s account. The company does not sell or license this data to third parties.
* **End‑to‑end encryption** – Data in transit between the headset, mobile device, and cloud is encrypted with TLS 1.3. At rest, it is encrypted using AES‑256.
* **Opt‑in analytics** – Users can choose to contribute anonymised, aggregated data to improve the underlying AI models. Participation is optional and can be revoked at any time.
* **Compliance** – Neurable’s privacy framework aligns with GDPR, CCPA, and HIPAA‑like safeguards for health‑related data, even though the headset is marketed as a wellness device rather than a medical device.

## Enterprise Angle

Beyond the consumer market, Neurable is courting large organisations seeking to boost employee wellbeing and productivity. Early pilots with a Fortune 500 software firm and a multinational consulting agency reported:

* **12 % rise in project completion speed** during weeks when employees used the headset’s focus alerts.
* **Reduced sick‑day usage** by 8 % over a three‑month trial, attributed to proactive burnout mitigation.

Ramses Alcaide summed up the corporate proposition: “It’s because we know we’re going to get higher productivity per employee; they’re going to be happier, they’re going to feel less burned out, it’s a win‑win for everybody.”

## Pricing, Availability & What’s Next

The Neurable One ships directly from Neurable’s website for **$499** (USD). Shipping is currently limited to North America, the EU, and select Asian markets, with a planned global rollout in early 2027. The company also offers a **“Starter Bundle”** that includes a premium leather case, an extra set of earpads, and a three‑month subscription to the Neurable Pro analytics tier (which adds custom brain‑training games and API access for developers).

Looking ahead, Neurable has hinted at a **future “Neurable Pro”** model featuring:

* Higher‑density sensor arrays (up to 24 electrodes).
* Integrated bone‑conduction audio for users who prefer open‑ear listening.
* Real‑time neurofeedback for VR/AR applications.

## Conclusion

Neurable’s decision to move from licensing to full‑stack hardware production marks a pivotal moment for consumer neurotechnology. By embedding 12 dry EEG sensors into a stylish, high‑fidelity headphone, the company delivers a seamless blend of audio performance and brain‑state awareness that feels less like a lab instrument and more like a daily productivity companion. Early research and enterprise pilots suggest tangible benefits—enhanced focus, lower burnout, and measurable happiness gains—while the firm’s strong privacy stance helps mitigate the usual concerns surrounding brain‑data collection.

Whether you’re a remote knowledge worker, a gamer looking for adaptive audio cues, or an organization aiming to nurture a healthier workforce, the Neurable One offers a compelling glimpse of how brain‑computer interfaces might soon become as commonplace as smartwatches.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to wear the headphones all day for the app to work?**  
A: No. The app can operate in “passive” mode, meaning it only records when the headset is on and EEG mode is enabled. You can switch to regular audio‑only mode at any time.

**Q: Are the EEG sensors comfortable for long‑term wear?**  
A: The sensors are dry electrodes embedded in soft nylon earpads. Most users report a “barely noticeable” sensation after the initial 5‑minute acclimation period.

**Q: Can I export my brain‑metric data?**  
A: Yes. The app provides CSV and PDF export options for all recorded metrics, as well as an API for developers on the Pro tier.

**Q: Is the device FDA‑cleared?**  
A: The Neurable One is classified as a wellness device, not a medical device, and therefore does not require FDA clearance. However, the underlying EEG technology complies with IEC 60601‑1 safety standards.

**Q: How does the headset handle ambient noise while the EEG sensors are active?**  
A: The six‑mic array works independently of the EEG front‑end, using beamforming to isolate voice commands and environmental sounds. ANC and transparency modes remain fully functional even when EEG sampling is on.

**Q: What is the warranty period?**  
A: Neurable offers a one‑year limited warranty covering manufacturing defects, with an optional extended warranty available for purchase.

**Q: Will future software updates improve accuracy?**  
A: Yes. Because the signal‑processing algorithms run on Neurable’s cloud, updates are delivered over‑the‑air and can refine metric accuracy without requiring hardware changes.
