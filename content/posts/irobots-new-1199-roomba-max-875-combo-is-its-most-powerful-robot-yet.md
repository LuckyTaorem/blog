## Introduction

At IFA 2026 in Berlin, iRobot announced two new Roomba models that promise to redefine the home‑cleaning landscape. The flagship **Roomba Max 875 Combo** ($1,199) is billed as the company’s “most powerful robot ever,” while the mid‑tier **Roomba Plus 678 Combo** ($899) offers a compelling blend of vacuuming and mopping. Both units feature the newly introduced **Auto Wash Dock** and **Seal Force Vacuuming Technology**, a system that dramatically enhances suction on carpets. This article dives into why these releases matter, the engineering behind their performance, and what they signal for the robotics industry.

## Why the Roomba Max 875 Matters

iRobot’s claim of the “most powerful robot ever” is not just marketing hyperbole. The Max 875’s **Seal Force Vacuuming Technology** lowers a hidden skirt inside the chassis to form a tight seal against carpet fibers, delivering a suction power of **35,000 Pascals**—the highest figure iRobot has ever published. For context, the previous generation’s top model operated at roughly 28,000 Pascals. This increase translates to noticeably deeper extraction of embedded dirt, pet hair, and dust mites, especially on high‑pile rugs.

The model also introduces **Ultrasonic Therma Mist Technology**, which continuously sprays heated mist during mopping. This feature softens dried stains and reduces the need for manual pre‑treatment. Combined with the **Power Spin Max Roller Mop**, which uses heated water at **300 RPM** and applies **18 Newtons** of downward pressure, the Max 875 can tackle stubborn grime that other robots struggle with.

From a consumer perspective, the Max 875’s advanced features address two pain points: carpet cleaning efficiency and surface protection. The robot automatically detects carpet, raises the mop, and deploys a protective shield to keep rugs dry, preventing water damage—a common complaint with earlier mopping models.

## Technical Deep Dive

### Seal Force Vacuuming Technology

- **Mechanism**: A retractable skirt inside the chassis lowers to create a seal against carpet fibers.
- **Suction**: 35,000 Pascals, up from 28,000 Pascals in the previous flagship.
- **Benefits**: Enhanced dust extraction, reduced re‑suction cycles, and lower energy consumption per cleaning cycle.

### Ultrasonic Therma Mist Technology

- **Operation**: Emits ultrasonic waves that generate a fine mist of heated water.
- **Temperature**: Maintains a consistent temperature to soften stains without damaging floor finishes.
- **Coverage**: Continuous mist during mopping ensures even distribution across the surface.

### Power Spin Max Roller Mop

- **Speed**: 300 RPM, higher than the 250 RPM found in the Plus 678.
- **Pressure**: 18 Newtons, calibrated to scrub without scratching delicate surfaces.
- **Water Management**: Integrated water reservoir with temperature control to maintain optimal cleaning fluid.

### Auto Wash Dock

- **Compatibility**: Included in both Max 875 and Plus 678.
- **Functionality**: Self‑cleaning mechanism that rinses and dries the mop head, extending its lifespan.
- **Connectivity**: Syncs with the iRobot app for maintenance reminders and usage analytics.

## Industry Impact

The introduction of the Max 875 and Plus 678 signals a broader shift in consumer expectations for autonomous cleaning devices. Key impacts include:

1. **Benchmarking Performance**: The 35,000‑Pascal suction sets a new industry standard, forcing competitors to revisit their vacuuming algorithms and hardware design.
2. **Feature Integration**: Combining advanced mopping (ultrasonic mist) with high‑power suction pushes the envelope for multifunctional robots, encouraging a move away from single‑purpose devices.
3. **Connectivity and Maintenance**: The Auto Wash Dock’s self‑cleaning capability aligns with the growing trend of “maintenance‑free” home appliances, reducing user intervention.
4. **Safety and Reliability**: The protective shield for carpets addresses a long‑standing safety concern, potentially influencing regulatory standards for consumer robotics.

## Future Outlook

Looking ahead, the Max 875’s architecture lays groundwork for several potential innovations:

- **Adaptive Cleaning Algorithms**: Machine learning could allow the robot to adjust suction and mopping intensity in real time based on floor type and dirt density.
- **Extended Battery Life**: With more efficient power usage from the Seal Force system, future models might achieve longer runtime or faster charging cycles.

These trajectories suggest that iRobot is positioning the Roomba line not just as a cleaning tool but as a core component of the connected home.

## FAQ

**Q: How does the Seal Force technology differ from previous suction systems?**  
A: It uses a retractable skirt to form a tight seal against carpet fibers, boosting suction to 35,000 Pascals—significantly higher than earlier models.

**Q: Will the ultrasonic mist damage delicate rugs?**  
A: No. The mist is heated to a controlled temperature and applied continuously, softening stains without harming fibers.

**Q: Is the Auto Wash Dock compatible with third‑party mop heads?**  
A: The dock is designed for iRobot’s proprietary mop heads; third‑party accessories may not fit securely.

**Q: How often should I replace the mop head?**  
A: iRobot recommends replacing the mop head every 6–12 months, depending on usage frequency and floor type.

**Q: Does the Roomba Max 875 support voice control?**  
A: Yes, it is compatible with major voice assistants such as Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri.

These innovations not only elevate the Roomba brand but also push the entire robotics industry toward more integrated, intelligent, and user‑centric solutions. As connectivity and security continue to shape consumer expectations, iRobot’s latest models are poised to lead the way in the evolving landscape of smart home automation.
