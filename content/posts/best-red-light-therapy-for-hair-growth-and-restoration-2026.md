## Why Red‑Light Therapy Matters for Hair Loss

Hair loss affects roughly 80 % of men and 40 % of women by age 50. Traditional treatments—minoxidil, finasteride, and surgical options—carry side‑effects or high costs. Red‑light therapy (LLLT) offers a non‑invasive, drug‑free alternative that harnesses photons to stimulate cellular activity. WIRED’s 16‑week, board‑certified dermatologist‑backed study confirms that consistent exposure to wavelengths between 630 nm and 680 nm can:

- **Activate dormant follicles** by increasing ATP production in dermal papilla cells.
- **Enhance microcirculation**, delivering oxygen and nutrients to the scalp.
- **Reduce inflammation** and lower dihydrotestosterone (DHT) locally, mitigating androgenetic alopecia.
- **Promote collagen synthesis**, improving scalp elasticity and hair shaft strength.

These mechanisms translate into measurable outcomes: new follicle emergence, thicker strands, and reduced breakage—exactly what patients report in the study.

### Wavelength Precision

The study underscores that 650 nm delivers the highest penetration depth (~2 mm) into the dermis, matching the location of hair follicles. Devices that deviate from this range—either too low (red‑green) or too high (infrared)—show diminished results.

### Power Density and Treatment Frequency

Power density (mW/cm²) and session frequency are critical. The Gro Well’s 3–4 J limit prevents oversaturation, allowing scalp recovery between sessions. Daily use of high‑power devices can cause thermal stress, as observed with the i Restore Elite’s heat buildup.

### Safety Considerations

All tested devices complied with ANSI safety standards for ocular and skin exposure. Users should avoid direct eye contact and follow manufacturer instructions on session length.
