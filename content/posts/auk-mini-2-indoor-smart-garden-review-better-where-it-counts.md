## Overview: Why the Auk Mini 2 Matters

The indoor‑garden market has exploded since the pandemic introduced a wave of compact, tech‑enabled grow kits. Auk, a Norwegian startup founded in 2021, entered the arena with the Auk 1 and later the Mini 1, a minimalist countertop system that quickly gained a cult following among urban chefs. However, early reviewers—including WIRED’s commerce editor Kat Merck—identified two persistent pain points: leggy, weak‑stem herbs caused by a light that spilled into the surrounding room, and a lack of any “vacation mode” to prevent overgrowth when users were away.

The Mini 2, released in summer 2024, is Auk’s direct response. It refines the aesthetic, upgrades the optics, and adds an optional app (Auk Connect) that introduces scheduling and a low‑light “slow mode.” For anyone who has tried the Mini 1, the question is whether these tweaks translate into a genuinely better growing experience or simply a prettier box. This review breaks down the hardware, software, and horticultural performance to answer that question.

## Design & Build Quality: Scandinavian Minimalism Gets an Upgrade

Auk’s design language has always leaned heavily on Scandinavian minimalism—clean lines, muted colors, and natural wood accents. The Mini 2 expands the palette beyond the original black and white tops, adding espresso, sage, and terra‑cotta finishes. The wooden bases, previously limited to oak and walnut cork, now come in beech and ash, giving the unit a slightly lighter visual weight.

* **Footprint:** The new chassis is 12 % smaller than the Mini 1, making it easier to fit on crowded countertops. Despite the reduced footprint, the water reservoir grows by 25 %, holding roughly 3.75 L of water versus the Mini 1’s 3 L.
* **Pot Size:** The plant pots are larger, allowing deeper root development. This is especially noticeable when using the optional Chili & Tomato Kit, which swaps three standard pots for two larger ones and includes longer dowels for taller fruiting plants.
* **Materials:** The shift from cork to solid beech/ash eliminates the “soft‑spot” feel of the original base and improves durability. The overall construction feels sturdier, and the unit still retains the sleek, low‑profile silhouette that makes it suitable for a modern kitchen or office desk.

From a design standpoint, the Mini 2 now feels like a product that could sit comfortably in an Architectural Digest spread—something Merck hinted at when she praised the original’s aesthetic but noted it fell short of high‑end interior design standards.

## Lighting System & App Integration: From Intrusive to Intelligent

### Optical Lens Redesign

The most glaring complaint about the Mini 1 was its “intrusive lighting” that illuminated the entire countertop. Auk tackled this with a new optical lens system that narrows the beam and directs photons straight onto the foliage. In practice, the light appears dimmer to the human eye—Merck captured the moment with the quote, “The light was so visibly dim (in a good way) that I nearly forgot it was on.” Yet the photosynthetic output is higher, as evidenced by bushier herbs and reduced legginess.

* **Adjustability:** Users can toggle the light on/off, dim it, or select one of two preset grow modes—“Herbs & Salads” (higher intensity, shorter cycles) or “Fruits & Veggies” (moderate intensity, longer cycles). Adjustments can be made via a physical button on the light bar or through the Auk Connect app.

### Auk Connect App

The app is optional; the garden works perfectly without it. When launched, the app auto‑detects the garden via Bluetooth, requiring no account creation. Key features include:

* **Vacation/Slow Mode:** Dims the LEDs to a low‑light setting, slowing photosynthesis and water uptake. This prevents the overgrowth that plagued the Mini 1 when users were away for a week or more.
* **Scheduling:** Users can set custom on/off times, aligning light cycles with personal routines or local sunrise/sunset patterns.
* **Grow Mode Selection:** One‑tap switching between herb‑focused and fruit‑focused lighting profiles.

The addition of an app brings the Mini 2 into the broader smart‑home ecosystem. While Auk does not currently integrate with Apple HomeKit or Google Home, the Bluetooth‑based control model mirrors the connectivity approach seen in other consumer hardware, such as the Apple Watch accessories discussed in [Upgrade: 6 Essential Apple Watch Accessories for 2026](https://ltdeveloperblogs.github.io/posts/6-best-apple-watch-accessories-to-upgrade-your-watch-2026). As smart‑home hubs become more ubiquitous, the groundwork laid by Auk’s app could enable future third‑party integrations.

## Growing Performance & Nutrient System: Real‑World Results

### Medium & Nutrients

Both Mini 1 and Mini 2 use coco coir as the growing medium—a sustainable alternative to peat moss that provides excellent aeration. The nutrient regimen remains unchanged: two bottles labeled “Grow” and “Flavour,” each dosed at two pumps per liter of water. Merck’s testing adhered strictly to these instructions, avoiding any third‑party additives.

A notable addition is the fine gravel topper that sits atop the coir. This layer forms a crust that supports stems, reducing the floppiness observed in the Mini 1’s basil and parsley. While applying the gravel can be messy, the visual improvement in plant stability is worth the effort.

### Comparative Growth

Merck cultivated Italian parsley and Genovese basil in both models under identical environmental conditions. The results were clear:

| Metric | Mini 1 | Mini 2 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Leaf density (per pot) | 45 % lower | 30 % higher |
| Stem length (average) | 12 cm (leggy) | 9 cm (compact) |
| Light spill (subjective) | High | Low |
| Overgrowth after 7‑day absence | Significant | Minimal (thanks to Slow Mode) |

The larger water reservoir also means the Mini 2 can sustain plants longer between refills, a subtle but valuable convenience for busy households.

### Water System

Both models retain a stagnant‑water design—no pump circulates the solution. Instead, mature plants absorb water directly from the reservoir, while the dot‑studded wheel indicator (red dot = empty) provides a quick visual cue. The lack of a pump eliminates mechanical failure points and keeps the unit quiet, a benefit for office environments.

## Market Position & Pricing: Value Proposition

When the Mini 1 launched in 2024, it carried a price tag of $259. The Mini 2 arrives at $229 for standard color combos and $249 for the premium espresso‑and‑walnut finish—a modest price reduction despite the larger reservoir and upgraded optics. The optional Chili & Tomato Kit adds $39, positioning the system as a modular platform that can grow everything from basil to small fruiting plants.

In a market where Aerogarden, Click & Grow, and other countertop growers sit between $150 and $300, Auk’s pricing is competitive. The added app and improved lighting give it a technological edge, while the Scandinavian design differentiates it aesthetically. For consumers already invested in the Apple ecosystem, the Mini 2’s Bluetooth connectivity feels familiar, echoing the seamless accessory pairing highlighted in the Apple Watch accessories guide.

## Future Outlook & Industry Impact

### Why It Matters for Smart‑Home Horticulture

The Mini 2 demonstrates that incremental hardware refinements—better lenses, larger reservoirs, and optional software—can dramatically improve user experience without overhauling the core product. As indoor gardening becomes a staple of urban living, manufacturers will likely follow Auk’s playbook: focus on light quality, integrate low‑effort app controls, and provide modular add‑ons for specialty crops.

### Sustainability & Environmental Impact

Auk’s use of coco coir—a byproduct of the coconut industry—aligns with growing consumer demand for sustainable materials. Unlike peat moss, which is harvested from fragile wetland ecosystems, coco coir is renewable and biodegradable. The absence of proprietary pods further reduces waste, as users can source their own seeds or reuse the included coco coir. However, the plastic components (pots, reservoir, and light housing) remain non-recyclable in most municipal programs, a common shortcoming in the indoor-gardening space.

## Verdict: Is the Auk Mini 2 Worth the Upgrade?

### For Mini 1 Owners

If you already own the Mini 1, the decision to upgrade hinges on your priorities:
- **Upgrade if:** You struggled with leggy plants, intrusive lighting, or overgrowth during vacations. The optical lens system and Slow Mode alone justify the switch.
- **Skip if:** You’re satisfied with your current setup and don’t need the app or color options. The Mini 1 remains a capable grower for herbs and leafy greens.

### For First-Time Buyers

The Mini 2 is a compelling entry into the indoor-gardening market, particularly for those who value design and ease of use. Its key advantages over competitors like Aerogarden include:
- **No mandatory subscriptions** (unlike Click & Grow’s proprietary pods).
- **Superior aesthetics** (fits seamlessly into modern interiors).
- **Modularity** (the Chili & Tomato Kit expands its capabilities).

However, it’s not without trade-offs:
- **No pump** (stagnant water may deter some users).
- **Limited height** (15-inch dowels still restrict tall plants).
- **App dependency for advanced features** (though not required for basic operation).

### Final Score: 8.5/10

The Auk Mini 2 earns high marks for its thoughtful refinements, particularly in lighting and app integration. While it doesn’t revolutionize indoor gardening, it sets a new standard for user experience in the countertop-garden category. For urban dwellers, renters, or anyone with limited outdoor space, it’s a worthy investment—provided you’re willing to accept its minor limitations.

## FAQ

### **1. Can I use my own seeds or pods with the Auk Mini 2?**
Yes. The Mini 2 uses coco coir, which is compatible with most seeds or seedlings. Auk does not require proprietary pods, though they offer starter kits for convenience.

### **2. How often do I need to refill the water reservoir?**
With mature plants (e.g., basil, parsley), expect to refill every **7–10 days**. The larger reservoir in the Mini 2 extends this interval compared to the Mini 1. The red dot on the water-level indicator signals when it’s time to refill.

### **3. Does the Auk Mini 2 work with smart-home ecosystems like Alexa or Google Home?**
Not yet. The Auk Connect app uses Bluetooth and does not currently integrate with voice assistants or smart-home hubs. Future updates may add this functionality.

### **4. What’s the warranty on the Auk Mini 2?**
Auk offers a **2-year limited warranty** covering manufacturing defects. The LED grow lights are rated for **50,000 hours** (approximately 5.7 years of continuous use).

### **5. Can I grow fruiting plants like strawberries or cherry tomatoes?**
Yes, but with caveats. The **Chili & Tomato Kit** ($39) includes larger pots and longer dowels to accommodate taller plants. However, the 15-inch height limit may still restrict some varieties. For best results, choose dwarf or compact cultivars.

### **6. How does the Mini 2 compare to the Aerogarden Harvest?**
| Feature               | Auk Mini 2               | Aerogarden Harvest       |
|-----------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Design**            | Scandinavian minimalism  | Functional, utilitarian  |
| **Lighting**          | Optical lens (less spill)| Standard LED panel       |
| **App Integration**   | Optional (Auk Connect)   | Proprietary app          |
| **Growing Medium**    | Coco coir (no pods)      | Proprietary seed pods    |
| **Price**             | $229–$249                | $150–$200                |
| **Height Limit**      | 15 inches                | 12 inches                |

**Choose Auk if:** You prioritize design, sustainability, and app flexibility.
**Choose Aerogarden if:** You want a lower upfront cost and don’t mind proprietary pods.

### **7. Is the gravel topper necessary?**
While not mandatory, the gravel topper significantly improves stem stability, especially for top-heavy plants like basil. It’s worth the minor mess during setup.

### **8. Can I use the Mini 2 outdoors?**
No. The unit is designed for indoor use only. Exposure to moisture, direct sunlight, or temperature extremes could damage the electronics.

### **9. How do I clean the Auk Mini 2?**
- **Reservoir:** Rinse with water and mild soap every **2–3 months** to prevent algae buildup.
- **Pots & Coco Coir:** Replace the coco coir between plantings. Wash pots with warm, soapy water.
- **Light Bar:** Wipe with a dry or slightly damp cloth. Avoid abrasive cleaners.
