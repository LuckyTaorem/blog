## Overview of the 2028 Mercedes‑AMG GT SUV

Mercedes‑AMG is set to rewrite the performance‑SUV playbook with the 2028 GT SUV, its inaugural full‑size electric SUV built entirely in‑house. The vehicle combines a staggering 1,150 horsepower output with ultra‑fast‑charging capability, promising sports‑car dynamics in a body that can comfortably seat a family. Built on the brand‑new AMG.EA architecture, the GT SUV is scheduled to arrive in the United States next summer, positioning AMG as a serious contender in the high‑performance EV segment.

Key highlights:

- **Powertrain** – 1,150 hp electric drivetrain delivering instantaneous torque.
- **Charging** – Proprietary ultra‑fast‑charging system targeting 80 % charge in under 20 minutes.
- **Architecture** – First AMG model on the dedicated AMG.EA platform, engineered for both agility and SUV practicality.
- **Production** – Fully in‑house development from chassis to battery pack, marking a strategic shift away from platform sharing.

## Technical Breakdown

### AMG.EA Architecture

The AMG.EA (Electric Architecture) is a modular, skateboard‑style platform that places the battery pack flat under the floor, lowering the centre of gravity and freeing up interior volume. Unlike previous AMG models that adapted existing Mercedes‑Benz platforms, AMG.EA is designed from the ground up for high‑performance electric applications.

- **Battery Capacity** – Estimated at 120 kWh, optimized for both range and power delivery.
- **Motor Layout** – Dual‑motor setup with a front and rear unit, each capable of 575 hp, enabling sophisticated torque vectoring.
- **Thermal Management** – Integrated liquid‑cooling loops for both battery and motors, ensuring consistent performance under track‑day conditions.

### Ultra‑Fast‑Charging System

Mercedes‑AMG has partnered with a leading charging network to develop a proprietary 350 kW DC fast‑charging protocol. The system uses a high‑current, low‑voltage architecture to reduce heat generation, allowing the GT SUV to replenish 80 % of its battery in roughly 18 minutes under ideal conditions.

- **Charging Curve** – Linear power delivery up to 300 kW, tapering to protect battery health.
- **Onboard Charger** – 22 kW AC capability for home charging, supporting both Type‑2 and CCS connectors.
- **Smart Integration** – Vehicle‑to‑grid (V2G) functionality that can feed excess energy back to the grid during peak demand.

### Performance Figures

While official acceleration numbers have not been released, extrapolations based on power and weight suggest a 0‑60 mph time in the low 2‑second range, rivaling the internal‑combustion GT R. The SUV’s aerodynamic package, featuring active grille shutters and a rear diffuser, helps maintain a drag coefficient near 0.30 despite its size.

## Why It Matters

### Redefining the Performance SUV Segment

The GT SUV arrives

the market at a time when luxury performance EVs are still a niche, and it does so with a blend of raw power and everyday usability that few rivals can match. By delivering over a megawatt of output in a full‑size SUV, AMG is challenging the notion that high‑performance electric cars must be low‑slung sports cars. The GT SUV also signals Mercedes‑AMG’s commitment to an all‑electric future, showing that the brand can retain its performance DNA while embracing new powertrain technology.

### Design & Aerodynamics

The exterior styling stays true to AMG’s aggressive GT lineage, featuring a wide‑track stance, a sculpted front fascia with a large active grille, and a rear spoiler that deploys automatically at higher speeds. The vehicle’s underbody is fully covered, and active aerodynamic elements—including an adjustable rear diffuser and front splitter—work together to keep drag low while generating downforce when needed. Despite its size, the SUV maintains a drag coefficient of approximately 0.30, a figure more typical of sleek coupes than traditional boxy SUVs.

Inside, the cabin merges luxury with a driver‑focused cockpit. A 15‑inch OLED central display runs the infotainment suite, while a secondary 12‑inch digital instrument cluster provides real‑time performance data, including torque vectoring maps and battery health. Premium Nappa leather, carbon‑fiber trim, and a configurable ambient lighting system create a high‑end ambience. Seating for five is complemented by a configurable rear bench that can fold flat to expand cargo capacity up to 1,800 L, making the GT SUV practical for both weekend getaways and daily errands.

### Sustainability & Technology

Beyond performance, AMG is emphasizing sustainability throughout the vehicle’s lifecycle. The battery pack incorporates a high proportion of recycled lithium‑ion cells, and the manufacturing process at the Mercedes‑Benz plant in Sindelfingen utilizes renewable energy sources for over 70 % of its electricity consumption. The GT SUV also supports over‑the‑air (OTA) updates, allowing the powertrain software to evolve post‑delivery, improving efficiency and unlocking new driving modes.

A standout feature is the integrated Vehicle‑to‑Grid (V2G) capability. When parked at home or at a compatible public charger, the SUV can discharge up to 20 kW back into the grid, providing owners with a potential revenue stream or a backup power source during outages. This bidirectional charging aligns with broader industry moves toward grid‑interactive EVs.

### Expected Pricing & Availability

Mercedes‑AMG has not released an official price tag, but analysts predict a starting MSRP in the US north of $150,000, given the vehicle’s technology suite and performance credentials. Pre‑orders are expected to open in early 2028, with first‑year allocations likely limited to the US, Europe, and select Asian markets. Deliveries are slated for the summer of 2028, aligning with the brand’s broader EV rollout schedule.

### Potential Challenges

- **Infrastructure:** While the 350 kW charging capability is impressive, the availability of compatible high‑power chargers remains limited in many regions, potentially bottlenecking the user experience.
- **Weight Management:** The large battery pack adds considerable mass, which could affect handling compared to lighter, smaller‑bodied performance SUVs. AMG’s advanced suspension and torque vectoring aim to mitigate this, but real‑world testing will be the true gauge.
- **Market Acceptance:** AMG’s core fan base has traditionally gravitated toward gasoline‑powered GT cars. Convincing these enthusiasts to transition to a fully electric platform will require compelling driving dynamics and brand messaging.

## Conclusion

The 2028 Mercedes‑AMG GT SUV represents a bold statement: high performance, electric power, and full‑size practicality can coexist without compromise. By leveraging the dedicated AMG.EA architecture, ultra‑fast‑charging technology, and a design language that stays true to AMG’s performance roots, the GT SUV is poised to set a new benchmark in the luxury performance SUV segment. If the promised specifications hold up under real‑world scrutiny, this model could redefine what consumers expect from an electric SUV and accelerate the broader industry shift toward electrified high‑performance vehicles.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to charge the GT SUV from 0 % to 80 %?**  
A: Using the proprietary 350 kW DC fast‑charging system, the battery can reach 80 % capacity in roughly 18 minutes under optimal conditions.

**Q: Will the GT SUV support third‑party charging networks?**  
A: Yes. While AMG’s own fast‑charging stations will be the primary option for maximum speed, the vehicle is equipped with a CCS‑2 inlet compatible with most public DC chargers.

**Q: What is the estimated driving range?**  
A: Mercedes‑AMG projects a WLTP‑rated range of about 350 miles (560 km) on a full charge, though real‑world figures will vary based on driving style and conditions.

**Q: Does the vehicle offer any off‑road capability?**  
A: The GT SUV includes an adjustable air‑suspension system with selectable ride heights and an all‑wheel‑drive system with torque vectoring, giving it competent light‑off‑road ability while maintaining on‑road performance.

**Q: Is there a performance‑oriented driving mode?**  
A: Yes. The “GT‑Track” mode unlocks the full 1,150 hp output, sharpens throttle response, and activates the most aggressive torque‑vectoring settings for track‑day use.

**Q: When will the vehicle be available for test drives?**  
A: Mercedes‑AMG plans to host exclusive preview events and test‑drive sessions at select dealerships in the US and Europe during the spring of 2028, ahead of the official summer launch.

**Q: Will there be any special editions?**  
A: AMG has hinted at limited‑run “GT SUV Performance” and “GT SUV Heritage” editions that may feature unique paint schemes, carbon‑fiber exterior accents, and bespoke interior trims, but details are still under wraps.
